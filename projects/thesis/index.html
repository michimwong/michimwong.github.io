Foreclosing the Argument

A Computational Approach to Thought-Terminating Clichés in Online Discourse

Michi Wong · MSc Social Data Science Thesis, Oxford Internet Institute · August 2026
Supervised by Bernie Hogan and Mohsen Mosleh

Abstract

Thought-terminating clichés (TTCs) are loaded, formulaic phrases whose function in an exchange is to foreclose inquiry rather than advance it. Despite their prevalence in online discourse, TTCs have received little systematic empirical attention, and existing literatures—computational natural language processing (NLP) and sociolinguistic research alike—collapse annotator disagreement into a single gold label before asking whether the construct can be reliably applied at all. This thesis addresses that gap by inverting the typical priority of detection over annotation: instead of training a classifier to recover a fixed ground-truth label, this study treats the annotation decision itself as the object of analysis. Using a theory-derived codebook and a three property (loadedness, formulaicity, and thought-terminatingness) operationalization of the construct, 589 thread-contextualized comments from deliberative subreddits were double-annotated by 83 trained and gate-screened humans as well as labeled by 12 LLM configurations spanning three providers and four variants. Under a perspectivist framework that treats annotator disagreement as signal and not error, agreement between humans, LLMs, and humans and LLMs is assessed using chance-corrected symmetric metrics that do not designate a ground truth label set. An additional battery of 58 intrinsic and relational text features is used to locate and compare where each annotator population draws its class boundary. This thesis finds that agreement is low between humans, but notes that this measure is consistent with similarly interpretive constructs. Models display higher inter-annotator agreement in both label and TTC-triggering span. Both populations anchor the positive label in the same affective region, yet whether they share a boundary of disagreement remains unresolved. This thesis ultimately contributes an agreement and construct boundary analysis of a theoretically significant but methodologically overlooked subjective construct.
